# RoadVerdict — SEO Strategy

**For:** the SEO agency or contractor taking on this account, and the RoadVerdict team.
**Version:** 2.1 — 2 October 2026. Replaces the 14 September 2026 handover.
**What changed:** version 2 (27 September) rebuilt the strategy from real ranking, indexing and competitor evidence
rather than assumptions, and shipped the technical fixes it called for (Section 9). Version 2.1 adds where per-model
data will come from and a five-model motorcycle pilot (Section 8), a data partner for links (Section 7), a fifth
pillar for the documented service history (Section 4), and judges pages by sign-ups rather than visits (Section 10).

---

## 1. Summary

- **The problem is authority and site structure, not keyword choice.** RoadVerdict ranks for 56 keywords, all at
  positions 48–83 (pages 5–9). The keywords are easy — difficulty 4–24 — but a new domain with almost no links pointing
  at it, whose own navigation hid half its pages, can't reach page 1 for anything yet.
- **The car pages were close to invisible — to visitors and to Google.** The header linked only to the motorcycle
  tools, on every page; the homepage didn't link to `/cars`, `/motorcycles` or any car page at all. Seven key pages sat in
  Search Console as "Discovered – currently not indexed". Fixed (Section 9).
- **Motorcycles are the winnable niche now; cars are crowded.** Motorcycle cost searches return forum threads and thin
  pages, and there is no free "is this motorcycle quote fair" tool. Car searches are owned by RAC, Checkatrade and
  booking marketplaces, and several dedicated car quote checkers launched in 2026.
- **So: win motorcycles first, and enter cars through the long tail** — specific jobs, sourced prices, the independent
  verdict — rather than head terms like "car service cost".
- **Links are now the biggest lever.** Every tactic below is secondary to getting other sites to link to RoadVerdict.

## 3. Competitive landscape

### Cars — crowded

- **Head terms** ("car service cost uk", "brake pads cost", "mot cost"): RAC, Checkatrade, Bumper, Kwik Fit and similar,
  plus a 2026 wave of cost-guide sites publishing "[job] cost UK 2026" pages.
- **"Is my quote fair"**: several free car tools now exist — MyRepairCost (per-model prices for about 1,095 cars and
  vans), GarageRates and Kwoute (upload a quote, compare to regional rates), AutoFixFair — alongside booking
  marketplaces (ClickMechanic, FixMyCar/WhoCanFixMyCar, Fixter, Bumper) that earn commission on the repair.

A new domain will not outrank these on car head terms in the next six months.

### Motorcycles — open

For "motorcycle service cost UK" and "is my motorbike service quote fair", page 1 is forum threads, a car-import
knowledge base, breakdown-cover blog posts, a tyre retailer and SortMyBike (a quote marketplace). No one offers a free,
sourced, instant verdict on a motorcycle quote. Semrush difficulty of 4–24 on the terms we already rank for confirms
it.

### What RoadVerdict has that competitors don't

1. **Motorcycles and cars, equally.** Every car quote checker above is car/van only.
2. **Sourced, honest figures.** Every price names its source and date and shows how well sourced it is — unlike
   unsourced "2026 cost guides".
3. **Independent.** RoadVerdict doesn't sell the repair, so it has no reason to push you towards a garage.
4. **The whole ownership loop.** Quote checking, buying checks, running costs, a service history that transfers to the
   next owner, and a shareable documented history for sellers — no competitor connects these.

## 4. Positioning

**RoadVerdict is the free, independent, UK-specific verdict for the moments before you spend money on a motorcycle or
car** — paying a garage, buying a used vehicle, or deciding whether you can afford to run one — **backed by sourced
figures, not guesses.**

Every page, keyword and link-building angle should reinforce one of the four pillars:

1. **The fair-price verdict** — quote checker and price guides.
2. **The buyer's truth** — buying guide, free MOT history and valuation, optional paid history check.
3. **The true cost** — cost calculators and ownership guides.
4. **Motorcycles as a first-class vehicle** — the niche RoadVerdict can own first.
5. **The documented history** — receipts turned into a dated service history that goes with the vehicle when it's
   sold. Never call it "verified": it's the owner's own record, with receipts attached.

These map onto the four moments the product is sold on: before paying a garage (1), before buying (2), while owning
(3, 5) and when selling (5). Each search entry point should lead to the tool for its moment, not to a general
homepage pitch.

## 5. Strategy — win motorcycles, enter cars through the long tail

**Priority order:**

1. **Authority (links)** — the only thing that moves positions 48–83 to page 1 (Section 7).
2. **Get every existing page indexed and ranking** — motorcycle tools and hubs first, where competition is weakest.
3. **Price guides** — one page per job, sourced, for both vehicle types. Motorcycle guides should rank first; car guides
   target long-tail variants ("interim service cost small car", "do I need new discs") rather than head terms.
4. **Car long tail** — specific questions and comparisons where the big sites are generic.
5. **Linkable data assets** — once there's traffic worth amplifying.

What we are deliberately **not** doing: chasing "free car check", "MOT history" or "HPI check" head terms (GOV.UK and
large brands own them), or publishing per-model pages before we have per-model data.

## 7. Authority — the link-building plan

Search engines treat links from other sites as votes. RoadVerdict has almost none, which is why difficulty-4 keywords
still sit on page 6. This is the single most important section of this document.

**Where the links come from:**

1. **Motorcycle communities (first).** UK bike forums, owners' clubs for popular models, r/motorcycles and
   r/CarTalkUK-style subreddits, and Facebook groups. The motorcycle quote checker and price guides answer questions
   those communities ask every week. Answer genuinely; link only where it helps.
2. **Our own channels.** TikTok, YouTube and Instagram bios and descriptions linking to specific tool pages, not just
   the homepage — and tagged (`?utm_source=tiktok`) so Cloudflare shows which ones bring visitors.
3. **Owners' clubs and model-specific sites.** A short, honest pitch: a free, independent quote checker and cost
   calculator for their members' bikes.
4. **Resource pages.** "Useful links for new riders", rider-training schools, bike-insurance comparison blogs.
5. **Linkable data (from month three).** An annual "True cost of UK motorcycling" report from our own sourced data —
   something journalists and bloggers cite.
6. **Data partners.** Bike Reliability (bikereliability.co.uk) publishes MOT pass rates for every motorcycle make and
   model from DVSA data, free to cite under CC BY 4.0 with a link, and covers no costs at all. We cite its figures on
   per-model pages (Section 8); it is a natural site to link to our quote checker and running-cost pages, since we
   answer the cost question its visitors have next.

**Targets:** 10 genuine referring domains in 60 days; 25 in 120 days. Quality over quantity — no paid links, link
farms or directory spam.

## 8. Content plan

### Shipped (27 September 2026)

- **Price guides — 12 pages plus 2 hubs:**
  - motorcycles: full service, basic service, tyres, front brake pads, chain and sprockets, MOT
  - cars: full service, interim service, oil change, front brake pads, tyres, MOT
  - each has sourced prices by size, a confidence label per figure, what's included, what moves the price, red flags,
    FAQs, and a "Check my quote" button that opens the quote checker with the job pre-selected
- **Cornerstone guides (September 2026):** buying a used motorcycle/car; the cost of owning a motorcycle/car.

### Next (in order)

1. **More price guides, once sourced data exists.** Cars: clutch, cambelt/timing belt, brake discs, battery, air-con
   regas. Motorcycles: valve clearance check, fork seals, battery. The rule: a new job gets a page only after it has
   sourced rows in the price tables (which also makes it available in the quote checker).
2. **The documented-history guides** — "how to prove service history when selling a motorcycle or car" and "how to
   rebuild a lost service history" (ask the dealer and garages for copies, use the MOT history, photograph the
   receipts). Low competition, and they lead straight to receipt scanning and the shareable history link.
3. **Motorcycle-first explainers** — "basic vs full motorcycle service", "why valve checks cost so much", "first year
   of motorcycle ownership: real costs".
4. **Per-model pages — gather the data first, then a five-model motorcycle pilot.** Our prices are by size class, not
   make and model, and "Honda CB500F service cost" pages built from class-level data would be thin content in Google's
   eyes. Searches like "Yamaha MT-09 service cost" are still where a small site can win, so the work is collecting real
   per-model data:
   - **Dealer service menus.** Many UK dealers publish fixed prices per model and service interval. Three to five
     dealers per model, each dated and linked, gives a sourced range for each service.
   - **Manufacturer service schedules.** Intervals and what each service includes (valve checks especially), from
     the owner's manual.
   - **DVSA MOT data.** The anonymised MOT results (Open Government Licence, data.gov.uk) give the common failure
     reasons and typical yearly mileage per model; Bike Reliability's per-model pass rates can be cited with a link.
   - **Owners' own costs**, aggregated and anonymised, only once a model has enough owners (10 or more) for the
     figure to mean something.
   - **First-hand ownership.** The first page can be the founder's own bike: what it has really cost to run, from its
     receipts. Google rewards first-hand experience, and owners' groups share pages like that.

   **The pilot:** five popular UK motorcycles, chosen by Semrush volume. Each page: the service schedule, sourced
   dealer prices per service, common MOT failure points, what moves the price, "Got a quote? Check it" and "Track
   your bike's real costs". Measure for six to eight weeks; build more only if they get indexed and start ranking.
   Cars follow later through the long tail (for example a specific model's timing-belt or brake job), not head terms.

### Rules for every new page

- Every price comes from the sourced benchmark tables, with source, date and confidence shown. No invented figures.
- Written for the specific job and vehicle, not a template with words swapped.
- Uses `pageMetadata()` for its title, description, canonical URL and share tags, and appears in the sitemap and the
  footer directory (both generated from `PRICE_GUIDES`).
- When the apps are out, pages point to them with a quiet text link where it helps ("Keep this bike's history in your
  pocket"), never a pop-up or interstitial — Google demotes intrusive interstitials on mobile.

## 9. Technical SEO — status

**Fixed 27 September 2026:**

- **Internal linking.**
  - The header now leads with Motorcycles | Cars | Quote Checker | Guides & Prices | Pro; the quote-checker link
    follows the vehicle of the page you're on.
  - A footer directory links every public page from every page.
  - The homepage links to both vehicle hubs and both sets of price guides.
  - Every tool page links to its twin for the other vehicle and to that vehicle's price guides.
- **robots.txt.** A stale static robots.txt left on the server by an early deploy had been overriding the real policy,
  which left the private report and transfer links and `/tomasz` crawlable. robots.txt is now answered by the
  middleware, and a post-deploy test checks the live file on every deploy.
- **Share tags.** Every page used to send the homepage's `og:url`, title and description, so links shared on Facebook,
  WhatsApp, LinkedIn or Reddit previewed as the homepage — and Facebook credited the share to it. Each page now sends its
  own.
- **Titles and descriptions.** The main pages are rewritten around what people search: "quote checker", "running cost
  calculator", "used car buying guide & free car check".
- **Sitemap.** Every page used to report "modified now" on every request, a signal Google ignores. Each page now carries
  its real last-changed date, and price guides are listed automatically.
- **llms.txt.** Now lists the car tools and the price guides.
- **Structured data.** Breadcrumbs on the new pages (Home › Cars › Car price guides › …), FAQPage answers taken from the
  same numbers as the visible table, and an ItemList on the price-guide hubs.

**Still open:**

- The stale `robots.txt` and other leftover files (`hero-road.jpg`, `road-backdrop.jpg`) are still in the server's
  `wwwroot/public`. They're harmless now, but worth deleting via Kudu or the Azure portal.
- Car per-model data (see Section 8).
- Core Web Vitals: mobile LCP and Speed Index were improved in September; re-check in PageSpeed Insights once the new
  pages are indexed.

## 10. Measurement

**Tools:** Google Search Console (Google search), Cloudflare Web Analytics (all visits and referrers), Semrush (rank
tracking — track the 56 keywords above plus the car targets once validated).

**Targets:**

| By | Target |
|---|---|
| 30 days (end of Oct 2026) | All tool pages, hubs and price guides indexed; 10+ motorcycle keywords in the top 30 |
| 60 days | 5+ motorcycle keywords on page 1; first car price guide in the top 30; 10 referring domains |
| 120 days | Motorcycle price guides on page 1 for their primary terms; organic search a clear top-3 traffic source in Cloudflare; 25 referring domains |

**Monthly check:** Search Console queries report → new question-style searches become FAQ entries or new pages.

**Judge pages by sign-ups, not visits.** Tag links from our own channels (`?utm_source=` or `?src=`); the sign-up
funnel in the admin panel counts each step by source, and the activation panel shows how many people go on to add
something real. A page that brings 50 visitors and two owners who log their bike beats one that brings 500 and none.

## 12. Boundaries

- UK only. All copy must stay accurate to what's live; prices must come from the sourced tables.
- The quote checker's brand and region adjustments are estimates for one specific vehicle; published price-guide tables
  show only the base, sourced figures.
- No paid links, no scaled template content, no fake reviews or testimonials.
